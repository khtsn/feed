BC.Game CEO: "Our Asian players are the ones with the longest contracts, we are here to stay"

BC.Game CEO Ali "Ako" Muhanned has endured a whirlwind few days, experiencing the tumult of his team finding themselves disqualified from Major contention following Valve's Major rulebook adjustment, only to be reinstated by an exception a day later.

Ako hopped on the All About Counter-Strike podcast, with James "⁠BanKs⁠" Banks and Jonatan "⁠Devilwalk⁠" Lundberg, to give his experience of the last few days and offer some insight into what information he gave Valve when seeking an exception.

Valve issues exception for BC.Game to qualify for Singapore Major through Asia

Ako began by detailing the process he undertook to appeal Valve's decision to disqualify any teams who region-swapped, crediting HLTV's own Milan "⁠Striker⁠" Švejda with helping him get in touch with Valve. "It's not like an appeal where you get a document and you need to send in stuff," he began.

"I would say HLTV's Striker was very good with explaining the situation, I must give him credit for that. Also I must say they are… they are neutral in everything, not taking any sides. They are like 'this is what we know, this is what you must do to move forward with getting in touch with them [Valve]."

We reached out to them [Valve], emailed them, just told them the truth

Ali "Ako" Muhanned

Ako went on to explain that he believes BC.Game's situation did not match up with the reasons Valve had stepped in to address region-swapping. "We understood it was more about abusing the system, dealing with the dual citizenships, terminating players' contracts and not giving them fair chances, abusing it that way," he said.

"We made our case pretty clear, we showed what our plan was. We reached out to them [Valve], emailed them, just told them the truth, showed them some stuff. All of our players are on long contracts, our Asian players are actually the ones with the longest contracts, so we are here to stay."

BC.Game CEO asks for fresh start: "Take all our points away"

BanKs later asked if Ako thought that his bold offer to start from scratch, an offer made in an open letter posted to X, in the Asia region helped his team's case. According to Ako, it showed Valve the mentality behind BC.Game's approach.

"I think for sure they understand that we're not here for the money, or trying to fuck over a player to make the Major. The whole thing with us taking that risk is we are showing that we are here to stay. It doesn't matter if we make the Major or not, that's the reality. We just want to be treated the same, and fair."

I think for sure they [Valve] understand that we're not here for the money

Ali "Ako" Muhanned

Ako also gave some insight into the reason he proposed a fresh start in the Asia VRS, rather than asking to switch back to Europe, and it had all to do with Tyson "⁠asap⁠" Paterson. "We would go back and play EU, meaning that asap would be removed," he began.

"I shut that down directly in the call, I said that's not gonna happen, that's my promise I gave him[…] I gave him a promise, we're gonna keep it. We're lucky that we don't need the Major to survive, that's the reality, and I'd rather stand on my word that we're not going to remove him just because we have the chance to make the Major."
