flameZ: "We believe we can get back to [our best]"

We caught up with Shahar "⁠flameZ⁠" Shushan after Vitality got their first victory at StarLadder StarSeries Fall over magic and proceeded to the upper bracket semi-final, where they will face the winner of FURIA vs. MIBR.

The Israeli rifler spoke about how Vitality is navigating an uncertain season after a lot of success, and how they're working to push for a Stage 3 berth at the Major with the biggest prize of the season in their crosshairs.

"The circumstances are tough, and if we get wins, if we get to the Major in Stage 3, we know we're going to come to the Major very prepped," the 23-year-old says, "and we will have a chance to play really well at the Major, so that's the main goal."

Listen to the interview here.

Tell me a little bit about Vitality trying to get back on track, right? You guys just played an event with jL. You're going to play a little bit more with jL later as well. So tell me about the complications that this brings with it.

Well, it's tough because you have the importance of the VRS, but you know that some tournaments you're not going to be that strong because you don't have the full roster, and it's going to be tough to practice because William [mezii] took Porto off, and he couldn't pracc for a bit after that because of the baby. Then Dan [apEX] is going to have his baby, around PGL. Obviously, we cannot pracc with him before that, and then we probably practice with jL, but it's very different.

You've got to get used to a new voice calling and get used to a new player in the server in a different position. Even though we know Justin [jL], he can play a lot of different positions. So that complicates the practice and the path to just grind it out because it's just so weird to get a player out, switch with another player. At least we know Justin. That's the positive thing.

So what's the goal? Is it to just sort of go as far as you can, kind of keep yourselves up there as well as you can, and try to have a push before the Major when the gang gets back together?

Well, what we're looking for right now is just to try to get to our form, to compete a bit stronger. Some of the games we've lost this season, we haven't shown our big game even, and we don't care if we lose a tournament or we win a tournament if we didn't play good CS. We want to play good CS.

Of course we care if we win, but if we lose a tournament and we played bad, we feel really worse than if we played good and the enemy played really well. And that was the case this season, so we just want to get back into this form, and that's the main goal. If we do that, we'll get the trophies, and we'll get all the things we know we can get.

Yeah, you speak of the situation with Justin. It's a funny one because you do know him, but he's going to come back for a different player, so that's going to kind of mix things up. Is there something from your first stint with him that he added or brought, or made you think, or did it work anything out in that sense?

It forces you to focus on the basics a bit more, not to focus on the playbook, which happened. We focused a lot on just trading each other, doing very basic stuff, but trying to do it well. I think the one key thing about him is that he's such a good energy bubble. So in the game, it creates a different energy. It's a bit more fresh, a lot of jokes, a lot of weird... like we have a lot more fun in the server. But on the other end, he does bring a lot of his movement. He moves a lot. He's a player that wants to move to make plays, so he does bring his game into it, and we need to kind of adapt as well as he adapts to us.

But I think it's going to be much different because we don't have apEX, so it's going to be really different for him because mezii's going to call a bit differently. We're going to have maybe very different positions and stuff like that.

So I think for him, for all of the roster, it's going to be a bit weird to do that, but at least we have more time to pracc. With Justin right now, we had, I think, two days of practice, the media day and another day because it was EWC to Copenhagen, so we're going to have a bit more time to pracc, a bit more time to get aligned on stuff that we need to do.

And looking a little bit more at the bigger picture, you guys established your era, we can call it, and then you had such a high bar, and you were winning so much. Now, navigating through all of this, how do you kind of look at getting back to that? Because there's all these things happening and all these changes going on, but at the same time, are your best days behind you? Can you go back to them?

Well, we believe we can get back to that because in 2025 we had a tough beginning of the season, or the second season, and we did come back to it, and we won the Major, and then we won another five trophies in the beginning of the year. So we do believe in everyone. We do believe in this roster. I don't think there is anyone that believes that there is a better roster to be in other than this group of five people.

It's just all about grinding and finding our form again, and sometimes those moments are required for you to be the best again, and we take those moments, and maybe it's tough, but it's also a tough season with the standings. The circumstances are tough, and if we get wins, if we get to the Major in Stage 3, we know we're going to come to the Major very prepped, and we will have a chance to play really well at the Major, so that's the main goal.

Even if we don't win a trophy this season, if we win a Major, I think it's going to create a very good feeling. But obviously, we're trying to compete for everything, and the goal is not the trophies. They're more of the outcome of just playing good Counter-Strike.

Yeah, and this event is also kind of different. It's like a studio event, eight teams. A little bit different from maybe a lot of the stages you're used to at this point, right? So what is it like for you guys coming back to a tournament like this and having a little bit more of a relaxed environment?

Well, it's a bit different. We don't like that, to be honest. We like the stage games. We like to be in front of the crowd. We sometimes miss it, right? But this event is just an opportunity to get a lot of VRS points, of course. It's been a while since we played StarLadder. Last time it was the Major, and it was a good event, so we're just excited to play. We just want to play good CS, get into form because we have, I think, two more tournaments with the whole roster this season or three more, including this one. So that's something that we just want to use those opportunities to play as best as we can, and that's about it.

As I was saying earlier, you've had that time at the top, and keeping that hunger, I guess, is a little bit hard. So now when you aren't playing at your best, is it helping you to feed that a little bit more again, perhaps?

Yeah, of course, for sure. If you're playing less good, if trophies are... I wouldn't say they were ever guaranteed, right, but they feel way harder than before. Then getting a trophy, even the smallest one, will mean a lot. But I do think we play the same, we try to put the energy in the game. We try to make our plans. We try to play the Counter-Strike we know.

So it's not a difference in this sense, but it's more about prepping for the tournament, coming to an event with a certain mindset, not wanting to lose even the smallest round, in which in the past maybe we'd troll a bit, lose a round, and then somehow would win the game and not look at it this round ever. But nowadays we try to work on many things and just being aligned, playing together as much as we can, and that's it.
