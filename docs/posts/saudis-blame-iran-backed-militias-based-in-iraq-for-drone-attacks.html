Saudis Blame Iran-Backed Militias Based in Iraq for Drone Attacks

Although Iraqi-based militias have not publicly claimed responsibility for attacks since hostilities resumed this month, Gulf states had previously reported intercepting incoming fire from proxy groups during the early phase of the war.

Separately, Houthi rebels said on Monday that they had targeted crude oil supplies and transport routes linking Saudi Arabia’s eastern fields to the Red Sea hub of Yanbu using several drones. The Saudi defense ministry has not yet addressed that claim.

The Houthis form part of Iran’s broader “axis of resistance” alongside Iraqi factions, Hamas in Gaza and Hezbollah in Lebanon.

The wider region remained on edge on Monday as Jordan and Israel said they had intercepted incoming drones. Israeli officials said two drones had been intercepted near the Jordanian border and that the authorities were investigating where they had been launched from.

The fresh strikes follow a two-week stretch during which Iran targeted sites in Bahrain, Kuwait and Jordan in response to daily American air raids. Direct strikes have cooled over the past 48 hours, with the Pentagon halting its bombardment after 13 consecutive nights and no new reports of Iranian attacks against neighboring states.
