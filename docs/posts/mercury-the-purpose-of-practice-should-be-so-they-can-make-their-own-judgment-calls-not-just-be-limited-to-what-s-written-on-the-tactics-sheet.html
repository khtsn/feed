Mercury: "The purpose of practice should be so they can make their own judgment calls, not just be limited to what's written on the tactics sheet"

TYLOO overtook The MongolZ this month to become Asia's number one team, but the Chinese team's in-game leader is aware that there is plenty left to do to secure the region's only spot in Stage 2 of the Major.

25-year-old Jingxiang "⁠Mercury⁠" Wang has been the team's captain since the summer of 2024, and he admits that the position comes with a lot of pressure.

So, does he enjoy that pressure? "I do enjoy it," he said. "But sometimes when we lose some really crucial matches, I do doubt myself a bit, but it's mainly up to me to adjust my mental state."

We also asked Mercury about WeiJie "⁠zhokiNg⁠" Zhong's comments from our last interview with TYLOO, where he said that game sense was the team's biggest weakness compared to top teams.

It's something the team are trying to address: "The purpose of practice," Mercury explains, "is so that they can make their own judgment calls, not just be limited to what's written on the tactics sheet."

Editor's note: The interview was conducted via an interpreter, before Mercury's original answers were translated by HLTV.

Mercury, welcome to Pro League...

Jee walks past

HLTV? Mr. Mercury! [laughs]

...First of all, I wanted your reaction on becoming number one in Asia on the VRS.

What are my feelings? It's mainly because Mongolz's ranking-match results are also really strong, and at the same time, for Asia No. 1, before the ranking cutoff there are two big events we still need to play. So for these tournaments, we'll try our best to perform well and earn as many points as possible, and try to compete with Mongolz for that Asia No. 1 spot, because being Asia No. 1 gets a direct invite to CD2 [Stage 2].

How important would stage two be for the team?

Because this time for the Major, in the Asia region, CD2 only has one spot, and the rest are all CD1, so I feel like CD2 means for us we can at least skip some of the CD1 matches, and go straight into CD2 to play.

Sometimes when we lose some really crucial matches, I do doubt myself a bit

And I wanted to ask about in-game leading. How do you feel as the in-game leader now? Does you feel like you are developing into the role?

After becoming the IGL, I actually feel like this position comes with a lot of pressure, and at the same time I have to put in more effort. And then, in these big tournaments, I need to do a lot of preparation before the match. At the same time, being the IGL means that for every match I have to play with a different style against different teams. Because if you always use the same style, it's really easy for the opponent to target it.

Do you enjoy that pressure?

I guess I do enjoy it. But sometimes when we lose some really crucial matches, I do doubt myself a bit, but it's mainly up to me to adjust my mental state.

How much of the style of the team is down to you versus the coach, versus the players? Does it really feel like your team in that way?

I'll explain how we're going to play this match, and at the same time the coach will give me some suggestions on tactics, and then I'll take in what he tells me and use it to prepare.

And zhokiNg, when he spoke to us, he was talking about the game sense of players in China compared to in, you know, tier-one Europe. What's the team doing to try and improve that?

In terms of players' individual skill level and improving overall game sense, I think it depends on regular practice, the purpose of practice. The purpose of practice should lean more towards default play, and let the players encounter those sudden situations themselves, so they can make their own judgment calls, not just be limited to what's written on the tactics sheet.
