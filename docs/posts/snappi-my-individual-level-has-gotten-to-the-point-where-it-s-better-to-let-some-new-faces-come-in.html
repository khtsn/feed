Snappi: "My individual level has gotten to the point where it's better to let some new faces come in"

With the announcement that Marco "⁠Snappi⁠" Pfeiffer has become the new head coach of HEROIC, time has been called on a playing career that spanned almost 20 years and included CS 1.6, CS:GO and CS2, so it was only fitting that HLTV Confirmed brought Snappi on to talk about his retirement.

The tenured Dane spent some time reflecting on his playing career with specific focus being given to last 18 months and how, over that time, he came to the decision it was time to take on a a new role.

Snappi takes over as HEROIC coach

"When CS2 came the first four or five events were good[…] but then afterwards it's been rough," Snappi admitted. "I think my individual level has gotten to the point where it's better to let some new faces come in."

"I was often frustrated that I couldn't get the output out that I was individually used to, and that maybe also impacted the calling to a certain degree. Obviously when you can use yourself a little bit more actively, it allows for different things."

Snappi professed to being "frustrated" with his individual level towards the end of his career

Snappi also admitted the pressure that accompanied his declining form may have affected his play even further. "The last six months I think I had a lot more pressure, because I knew I was playing… either I perform now or it is retirement, and that added a different level of pressure than I think I ever had in my career.

"Also the hate was fully out of control on all social media [laughs], it was like something I never experienced before, to that degree at least. What is it they say, 'you either live a hero or die a villain' or something like this, and I think in these social circles it definitely ended up like that."

The hate was fully out of control on all social media

The Dane then went on to explain who eventually took the decision for him to step down, revealing it was a relatively mutual affair between him and the organization. "Over the last months I started giving the idea to Xizt that maybe you need more firepower in my position," he began. "Obviously if you don't have the greatest stars, the firepower has to come from somewhere."

"In the end it was kind of mutual, of course. I think it was more of an org decision that came down, I don't think it was players involved in it at all, but I thought it was the right decision."

HEROIC is a strong team for Snappi to be taking the reins for in his first head coach role, and the 36-year-old is thrilled with the opportunity. "I thought it was a good opportunity for now, so I saw no reason to wait," he told the HLTV podcast crew.

Snappi is excited to take over a team he describes as "young with a lot of potential to grow"

"At first my plan was to wait for the transfer window after the Major, but when this opportunity came now, I just felt okay, I am gonna jump on it. I like that it's a young team with a lot of potential to grow, and I really like the people that I spoke to within the organization."

With an exciting new venture in HEROIC ahead of him, Snappi stated his confidence in his ability to adapt to coaching, so long as he maintains "a humble mindset."

I like that it's a young team with a lot of potential to grow

"I think there's a lot of things that I will need to learn," he began. "Maybe books that I will need to read, courses that I will need to take in order to understand certain psychological things that I might need to acquire, to be the best version of myself in this new role. I have to come in with a humble mindset.

"I also think I have an edge because I have been playing for so long, and I know how it feels to play in all three different games that there has been, pretty much. I've also been in so many team dynamics, both where it was good and bad."
