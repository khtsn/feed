JDC: "This is hopefully the year when we take the next step"

BIG locked up a spot in the Esports World Cup with a 2-1 victory over Sashi in what was otherwise a smooth qualifying run.

This is the second event of the season for BIG, who attended the XSE Pro League in China last month, finishing 5th-8th following a loss to PARIVISION in the quarterfinals. The team had a two-week break after the Chinese event before getting back to work roughly a week before the start of the EWC qualifier.

Speaking to HLTV after the qualifying match, Jon "⁠JDC⁠" de Castro said BIG are treating the EWC as a Major of sorts and that the tournament is a good test for the team as they look to make the leap into the elite category.

"Yeah, let's be real here: we have been talking in BIG, publicly as well, that we need to take the next step," he said. "We have been very successful in the tier-two, tier-three runs and tournaments. We've been winning them, but this is not our goal, so this is hopefully the year when we take the next step.

"And this means for us, of course, that any opponent we can face here, we need to be able to beat."

You are the second team to qualify for the Esports World Cup. In the end. it was not as clean as you would have liked, I guess. Talk to me about your run in this qualifier.

I think we started off very well. We went unbeaten until this game in maps and in series. But you always have to respect other teams. They know how to play Counter-Strike, they are working hard as well to get a chance for this, and it's the third game, the third best-of-three of the day, so comms might not be as sharp, the mood might be a little bit down because of energy, but it's just part of the game. As long as you can reset, it's a best-of-three, one lost map doesn't matter too much, and I think we did that today.

There was a lot of banter going on during this last match. Did you enjoy the setup, where you were playing facing each other, just a few rows apart?

Yeah, the banter started on the last map because they are a very scream-heavy team, and they like to do it. I think on Ancient we kind of got annoyed, to the degree where it was cheering us up to actually give them fire back. We were starting to get pissed off, so yeah, it was a lot of friendly banter, you can call it, but at the end of the day, we wanted to win really badly. So this is an old-school Counter-Strike environment. This is where I grew up back in the days, the same with tabseN, and it gave us kind of a nostalgic feeling for the event. It was pretty nice.

The makeup of the playoffs was very unpredictable with the random seeding. How comfortable and confident were you heading into the playoffs when you found out the kind of opponents that you could face in this qualifier?

Yeah, let's be real here: we have been talking in BIG, publicly as well, that we need to take the next step. We have been very successful in the tier-two, tier-three runs and tournaments. We've been winning them, but this is not our goal, so this is hopefully the year when we take the next step. And this means for us, of course, that any opponent we can face here, we need to be able to beat. At least that was our clear goal.

So I think our mindset is not that we are worried about who we face, but just that we show up and actually play our game. Because we know we are capable of beating all of those teams, but it's just a question if we show up or not.

After the event in China you had a short break, right? How much practice did you have heading into this one?

Yes. We took a good break because we needed it. We have been grinding, like I said, the first six months, no tournament pauses basically, trying to climb the VRS. So we had a good two weeks off. We had a week of practice before this tournament. I think it was a well-needed rest. Everyone on our team after this break has been way more creative, more energetic, and better in the game. I believe that sometimes less is more depending on the situation. And yeah, it helped us for sure.

You don't have any other major tournaments lined up at this moment. How important is this tournament for you in the grand scheme of things?

We are not completely sure what is up next. I don't think we, as players, are trying to look at it even. It's more of the management stuff. For us, this tournament is such a big tournament. In our eyes, it's probably one of the Majors. It is obviously not the Major, but it's a 2 million tournament, incredible teams, everyone is here. We want to prove that BIG is a good team and that we are here to play. So it's a Major for us.

And now it's the main event with all the big dogs. What are your expectations?

Our expectations are, of course, game by game, but, like I said, we are here to prove that we can beat better teams, so our expectations are, if we face those teams, to beat them. And we are there to push to the top 15, maybe top 10 in the world, and grind from there. So our expectations are game by game, but just win every game.
