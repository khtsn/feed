HUASOPEEK: "We're close to being a tier-one team, but I don't think we're there yet"

Ahead of their campaign in Suzhou, HUASOPEEK spoke to HLTV about the obstacles still facing 9z, what he has learned from watching Maksim "⁠kyousuke⁠" Lukin and Danil "⁠donk⁠" Kryshkovets, and the reaction in Chile to his MVP award.

Despite that individual milestone, his expectations of himself remain unchanged. For 9z, the immediate task is to regain confidence and return to the playoffs, with their ambitions extending beyond that first step.

After winning XSE Pro League Guangzhou 2026, you saw 9z were close to being a tier one team. What have the tournaments since then shown you about what's still missing?

I remember in Guangzhou, when I had my interview, I said we are close to tier one, but we have a lot of problems. In Paris, we lost against magic. These teams are hard for us, and to be a tier-one team, we need to win [against] these teams.

You mentioned that Cologne taught you a lot about your roles and entrying. What's a specific thing or things you do differently now because of that experience?

Now I'm looking at other entries, so I'm taking a lot of experience from how they do that job. And I think that helps me. I see a lot of kyousuke, I think he's a good entry, and also donk. So yeah, I think that helps me a lot.

You became the first Chilean player to win an HLTV MVP. What has the response back home been like? Is there a message or reaction that has stayed with you?

Yeah, a lot of people wrote to me about the MVP, and I'm the first [Chilean] MVP. Also, because in Chile we don't have a lot of Counter-Strike players. But yeah, I still receive messages from people who I never thought were going to write to me, like Chilean players from other games and that. So yeah, I'm also very happy because a lot of people wrote me.

Since winning your first MVP, has the way you judge your own performances changed? Do you set higher standards for yourself?

No, I think I have the same standards as always. I always think and do my job in the game, inside the game. And I think that award doesn't change me because it's just an award and just that. So I think I'm the same person [I was when I won the MVP].
