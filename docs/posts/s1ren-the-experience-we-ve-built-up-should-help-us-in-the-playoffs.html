Pavel "⁠S1ren⁠" Ogloblin took some time after the match to share his thoughts on BETBOOM's run through the group stage, what they need going into the playoffs in China, and the upcoming tournaments BETBOOM will attend.

This interview was conducted in Russian and translated by HLTV.

S1ren, three wins in the group stage, you're in the semi-finals. What are your impressions of your team's performance at this tournament?

Overall, after the Esports World Cup 2026, we worked quite a lot on our individual play in terms of better understanding the game, macro situations and things like that. And I think that's paying off now. That's it.

Tell me more about the matches, how they went from your point of view.

Honestly, the level of teams here isn't such that we thought we wouldn't make it to playoffs or anything like that, it's nice to do it, though. Overall, the match against BIG was pretty easy, even though, despite the scoreline, the scores were fairly close, it went very smoothly. We were leading the game and overall were favorites throughout the whole match. We didn't let any situation slip.

Against Astralis the game was already tougher, and you could feel some nerves there. Historically, we'd never beaten their tag before. And overall it was a pretty tough match, although in my personal opinion it should have gone a lot easier.

I don't think how you played in the group stage affects how you'll play in the playoffs

And today's game against MIBR, their first pick was fairly confident from us, except on CT, where it was a bit shaky. On T side we took it down pretty cleanly, fairly easily. Anubis was our pick, and it was a tough one. We'd played practice matches against them and knew they play Anubis well, but for us it was still the ideal pick, so we decided to take it. Overall the game was tough, but we ground it out.

Will your group stage performance help you enter the playoffs with confidence and show good form?

I don't think how you played in the group stage really affects how you'll play in the playoffs. Because we were in China a couple of months ago at Guangzhou and also went 3-0 out of the group, and then just got smashed 13-2, 13-2 by FaZe. So I think you need to approach the playoff match with a clear head, just like the next game, not think about past results, and just try to play your own game the same way we played it in the group stage.

Do you have confidence that you can compete with high-level opponents like FURIA, G2, Legacy?

I think if you look at our practice games, then we can definitely compete with them. But okay, that's clear, official games are a completely different story, and it'll also be on stage in front of a crowd. I think that will definitely affect us. But because over the last six months we've made it to playoffs and played on stage quite a lot, the experience we've built up should help us overall.

We're using this tournament as a ramp-up

You still have several more tier one tournaments coming up against much tougher opponents. Do you feel the importance of this tournament as a chance to gain points, or maybe even win it?

Yeah, one hundred percent we're using this tournament as a ramp-up, because literally in about twenty days we're heading off on a big trip through Europe for three tier S tournaments in a row. And overall this tournament serves as preparation for that marathon. Here we're trying to build up confidence so we can perform well there.

You weren't able to go to the Major. Tell me, what was that like for you?

That break was very, very hard on me mentally. I found out I wasn't going to the Major literally a day before the guys left for it, because my visa didn't come through. And mentally it was tough for me. During my little break I just tried to rest, get myself back together, and I went to a concert, just tried to relax, breathe out, and gather strength for the upcoming tournaments.

Overall there shouldn't be any visa problems in the future

Did it work? Did you manage to gather your strength, and maybe reflect on or reconsider anything in your game?

I think I understand what I need to work on, and probably even at that moment I understood what I needed to work on both as a person and as a player. I don't think the break itself really contributed to that, I think I just come to these realizations myself, both during tournaments and during time off. The break just helped me breathe out, recharge a bit, but mentally it was very hard missing the Major.

It isn't getting any easier for Russian citizens to get Schengen visas. Not sure how much you can comment on this, but how is the situation looking like for you guys right now?

I probably can't say much, but I can say that we've found a long-term solution, and overall there shouldn't be any visa problems in the future. That's it.
