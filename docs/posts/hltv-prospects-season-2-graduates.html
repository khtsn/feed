HLTV Prospects Season 2 graduates

dziugss ends his time on the list as the top prospect after FUT's breakout season

As we turn the page to a new season of HLTV Prospects, the first ranking of which will be published in the coming days, we look back at the players who stood out and are leaving the ranking this season, be it by graduating due to the number of maps played or by aging out.

HLTV Prospects Season 2 criteria, as of January 1, 2026:

Under 21 years of age

Of the 31 graduating prospects, 12 peaked inside the top 10 — our most elite group — nine of them inside the top 30, and ten among the top 50.

Of the graduates who peaked inside the top 10, the majority (7) graduated by playing more than 50 Big Event maps, one by playing over 100 Top 30 maps, one by playing over 300 Top 50 maps, and the rest (3) aged out.

The season started with Ninjas in Pyjamas's Kacper "⁠xKacpersky⁠" Gabara as the highest prospect on the list, a spot he held through March before Džiugas "⁠dziugss⁠" Steponavičius took over in April after FUT took the PGL Bucharest trophy. The former top player graduated through Top 30 maps played, while dziugss leaves the top spot open after clearing the 50 Big Event map threshold.

dziugss's FUT teammates leave the ranking in the same way; Dmytro "⁠dem0n⁠" Myroshnychenko having peaked at #2, Aulon "⁠Krabeni⁠" Fazlija at #6, and Nikita "⁠cmtry⁠" Samolotov at #8.

PARIVISION's Vladislav "⁠xiELO⁠" Lysov, who peaked at #3 at the beginning of the year, graduated on Big Event maps. His former teammate Emil "⁠nota⁠" Moskvitin, who also showed promise early on in the season during and after the BLAST Bounty Season 1 victory, but deflated with the rest of his team before getting benched in June, leaves the same way after reaching #7 in March.

Also in the group of players who played over 50 maps at Big Events is Anarbileg "⁠cobrazera⁠" Uuganbayar, who peaked at #4 and had an average rank of #18, although his future is currently in the balance after getting benched in The MongolZ.

William "⁠sirah⁠" Kjærsgaard's tier-two grind with 100 Thieves ends his time in the ranking with 325 maps in Top 50 matches. His teammate Alex "⁠poiii⁠" Nyholm Sundgren, who came into the team with less previous experience, remains.

Finally, João "⁠koala⁠" Pfeffer (#8 peak), Nikita "⁠tenzy⁠" Kochenyuk (#9 peak), and Adam "⁠adamb⁠" Ångström (#9 peak) graduate the list having turned 21 years of age.

The mid-range graduates start with Alan "⁠flayy⁠" Krupa, whose #13 peak and 9 appearances on the ranking lead the way. He leaves the list after turning 21, as do Adam "⁠Grizz⁠" Golden, who peaked at #20, Andreas "⁠aNdu⁠" Maasing, (#21 peak), Daniil "⁠Sdaim⁠" Tupov (#25 peak), Vladislav "⁠Alv⁠" Vorokhobov (#26 peak), Jacob "⁠jocab⁠" Nerheden (#27 peak) and Italo "⁠ksloks⁠" Meinberg (#28 peak).

Nikita "⁠jackasmo⁠" Skyba, who has been grinding in tier-two since 2023 with Passion UA and fnatic, leaves the list with five appearances and a #17 peak after amassing 352 Top 50 maps to his name.

His countryman Danylo "⁠s1zzi⁠" Vinnyk came out of nowhere this season to break out with B8. He made the list twice, with a personal best ranking of #24, and graduates from the list having put 74 Top 20 maps to his name since February.

Players who are graduating from the bottom 20 positions in the top 50 are all aging out, and the majority are from Asia and America.

Nicky "⁠NickyB⁠" Bruhn, Yalalt "⁠yAmi⁠" Oyunbileg, and Jaxon "⁠Peeping⁠" Cornwell leave with the most appearances of the bunch, with five each, followed by Lautaro "⁠guty⁠" Gomez with four. Guilherme "⁠levi⁠" Gustavo and Maks "⁠laxiee⁠" Andryei made it to the ranking three times.

Ozzy "⁠blood⁠" Rose and Henrique "⁠Machado⁠" Amaral had two appearances each, while James "⁠SaVage⁠" Savage made the list once, as did Evgeny "⁠Djon8⁠" Mudrenov.
