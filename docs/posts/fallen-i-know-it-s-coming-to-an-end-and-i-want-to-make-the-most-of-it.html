You faced G2 in the quarter-finals, where your run at FISSURE came to an end. Looking back at the series, where do you feel things started to get away from you, particularly on Dust2?

I think we lost some crucial rounds on the first map. It could have gone our way, and on Dust2 they simply played much better than us. We didn't have many chances there, so I guess we had to win the first map to actually have a chance in the series.

You also faced Legacy earlier in the tournament and came up short against them again. How did you process that result, and were there any recurring issues in the matchup that you found difficult to solve?

Playing against Legacy hasn't been easy. They have been playing very good Counter-Strike, and there were definitely lessons for us to take from that matchup ahead of future meetings with them.

You've had a demanding travel schedule recently, going from Paris to Porto and then China. How have you been managing it? Did you feel it had any effect on your energy, preparation, or performance at this event?

Those things are hard to judge because, of course, when you come to a tournament trying to win, it is easier to deal with all the travel. But it's definitely difficult to travel around every week, especially when the competitions are so far apart.

For us, it was important to come here to China. It was a good event to attend, and once it was decided that we would play, we were happy to come.

You still have several events coming up this season. How close do you feel the team is to putting together a title-winning run, and what still needs to improve for that to happen?

There are times when I feel we're not too far away from winning tournaments, but there are also times when it feels like we're actually very far away. It depends on the matches.

There are rounds we lose and situations we misread or don't play optimally. It's about finding the synergy required to play at such a high level that you can actually win tournaments, so we're working to find that synergy again.

But it's definitely a little different because I know it's coming to an end, and I want to make the most of it and do the best I can.

With your retirement now only a few months away, has that changed the way you experience tournaments? Do you find yourself appreciating these events differently?

It's always a mix of emotions to be in a situation like this because now there are only a couple of events left before it ends, right? I didn't think about this much before, but at the same time, I understand the duty I have to my team and how everyone relies on me putting in the work.

I try to put my emotions aside and work as well as I can every day to help the team. But it's definitely a little different because I know it's coming to an end, and I want to make the most of it and do the best I can.
