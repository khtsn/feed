MIBR chief: "insani has a good offer from us on the table; he's thinking it over"

EZOR CEO Alexandre "Kakavel" Peres has confirmed that star player Felipe "⁠insani⁠" Yuji has yet to commit his future to MIBR as he enters the final months of his contract with the Brazilian organization.

The 22-year-old is one of Brazil's most exciting talents but is still chasing his first notable trophy, almost four years after being promoted from MIBR's academy.

During the off-season, he was high on Legacy's shortlist as they looked for potential replacements for Vinicius "⁠n1ssim⁠" Pereira before the 25-year-old was reinstated to the starting lineup.

With insani's contract expiring in December, EZOR, the esports division of Spun Media, the company that acquired MIBR in October 2025, has already launched contract extension talks with the player.

"insani is a product of MIBR, and he's already shown us many times that he has a deep appreciation and gratitude, and is very happy at MIBR," Kakavel told Dust2 Brasil. "He's very important to us, and we’d really like insani to stay.

"I've had several talented players, and there have been times when a player has decided to go down a different path, right?

"What I can say about Insani is that he has a good offer from us on the table; he's thinking it over. He's already made it clear to me that he doesn't know what he wants for the future. I cannot say much so as not to disrupt his life while he makes the best decision for his future.

"And he knows that MIBR is an excellent choice for his future. And we really want him to stay, because we know how important insani is."

insani has been the one constant on MIBR's roster since 2023, becoming what Kakavel described as "the organization's big franchise player." Still, the EZOR chief admitted that they are already looking to a future without insani should the player opt to further his career elsewhere.

"What I can say is that we're going to do everything possible to keep him with us," he said. "But if that doesn't work out, we're simultaneously working on a plan for how to structure the team in case we lose insani, what steps we need to take if that happens. And I think we already have that structure in place today."

He added: "I don't know when insani will decide. He has the contract in his hands. We've been putting off talks lately because we had a few tournaments, then he took some time off during the player break. We agreed to talk again in the next few days to see if he'll sign or not."

MIBR run the risk of losing a marquee player for nothing at the end of the season, but Kakavel shot down suggestions that the organization should have sold the player in the summer to prevent that scenario,

"Think about what insani brings to the team," he said. "Weigh it all out and think about what’s more valuable. And keep in mind that we have four more months left in the year. And I have the chance to renew his contract during those four months.

"Right now I’m competing in several tournaments, my VRS rank is good, with a chance to improve it and compete in even more tournaments — and those tournaments generate revenue share. I have players I’ve brought in who want to play with him, and I’m preparing for 2027.

"What's the best way for me to handle this? With him on the team or without him? With him on the team, no doubt. So keeping him on the team is cheaper than the money people are offering me for him right now. I’ll lose money if I sell him right now. That’s how I see it."

MIBR, who were eliminated from the Esports World Cup in 17th-24th place, will return to action in the closed qualifier for IEM Beijing, scheduled for September 2-4.
