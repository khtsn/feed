nitr0: "Any win here would be huge for us"

NRG travelled to Barcelona looking to obtain some Valve points and make up ground in the race for a spot at the Singapore Major as they are currently ranked ninth in the Americas, only 36 points above SportsBetExpert.

Despite his team's perilous situation, Nick "⁠nitr0⁠" Cannella isn't too worried. With iBUYPOWER Masters FLM and FRAG University coming up after StarLadder StarSeries Fall, he believes NRG will have plenty of chances to solidify themselves in the ranking.

"There's a little bit of pressure, but, to be honest, we have some good tier-two tournaments coming up, and I know that for sure we can beat a lot of the opponents ahead of us, so I'm not really worried about that stuff," he said during media day.

'Captain America' also shared his thoughts on Håkon "⁠hallzerk⁠" Fjærli's addition to the squad, Jeorge "⁠Jeorge⁠" Endicott's return to the roster, what practice in North America has looked like, and the upcoming matchup against MOUZ.

Listen to the interview here.

This will be the first event of this stature for you guys this season. What do you make of your ramp up so far until now, and how is the team going into the event?

Overall, we're in a good spot considering the quality of practice we've had. It's probably the longest period we've had to practice since getting hallzerk on the team, given it's only NA practice, but it was still a decent period of time since FRAG St. Clair.

We've had time to fix a lot of mistakes and build chemistry, build up our team, our stratbook, and whatnot, so overall we feel good, but it's hard to be tested in North America. It'll be a bit different here, playing against tier-one teams, as opposed to just playing North American teams.

We already saw in the Liquid game that we were tested a lot more in our strats. When you make mistakes against better teams, obviously they punish you way more, and that's the biggest difference we noticed. Even playing Liquid, compared to other North American teams, so it's about being tested right now.

You're getting thrown straight into the deep end here. You're starting against MOUZ, one of the favorite teams at the event. What do you make of that particular match-up?

They're a good team, and they've made good changes, in my opinion. We played them in China, and we actually got a map off of them with daps playing when br0 had his personal issues. In terms of tier-one teams, they're more of a team that we understand, tactically and psychologically. Overall, they're a good matchup for us considering all of the tier-one teams. We're just going to have to play our best, try our best, and show our best game against them to win.

Jeorge is working harder for sure than he did in the previous iteration of the team, for obvious reasons

You say they made some good changes. You also made some changes. Tell me a bit about how bringing on Jeorge and hallzerk has changed the composition for the team after getting some practice in. How do you compare this NRG to the one we saw last season?

hallzerk has been a pretty easy implementation because he has been on previous North American teams like Complexity, and he gained a lot of experience playing with EliGE — my longest-tenured teammate — and we have the most experience together, so we think a lot [alike], and I can tell that he has a lot of similar game philosophy, terminology, stuff that we created on Liquid together. So it's been pretty easy to implement him, in that sense. He's been pretty consistent for us, in his attitude, his personality, in the game... So overall he's been a really good teammate, super happy with him.

Jeorge has improved a lot since we benched him, and it's nice to see. He's working harder for sure than he did in the previous iteration of the team, for obvious reasons. That's been nice. He's been doing all of our pistol rounds and stuff; giving him homework has been nice since he's the least experienced — to give him something to feel like he's giving something for the team. That's important for people. It's been going really well, and there's been a lot of positive attributes that they both brought, and the vibes are overall really good right now. It's been good.

You talk about the vibes being good and things clicking into place, but at the same time you're walking a tightrope on the VRS. You're in that fight for a Major spot. Are you feeling pressure from that?

There's a little bit of pressure, but, to be honest, we have some good tier-two tournaments coming up, and I know that for sure we can beat a lot of the opponents ahead of us, so I'm not really worried about that stuff.

You talk about these good tier-two events for you, the ones that get you through the VRS slog. But what expectations do you have here at a tournament like this?

We're just taking it match by match right now. We just need to get any dubs we can because we don't have much LAN bonus right now, and any win here would be huge for us, huge for the Major, and that's the main goal: just getting any victories we can get here and doing the best we can, making sure that the face we show is the best we can show, and making sure that we don't have any regrets after the tournament because you don't get many opportunities like this when you're struggling in the VRS system. Just getting any victories at this tournament would be huge for us.
