zhokiNg: "In China, the player pool is indeed too small; this might make the players in our team have a sense of security"

TYLOO exited FISSURE Playground 3 in 9-12th place after losses to Alliance and PARIVISION, and the team's coach, WeiJie "⁠zhokiNg⁠" Zhong, is not satisfied with the level his team showed.

"Our goal was actually to make it to the playoffs, but we didn't meet this expectation. And the whole process, I also feel the players didn't perform to the level they should have," zhokiNg said about TYLOO's performance.

He also touched on the reasons for the low number of roster changes in the organization, what TYLOO are missing to compete with the world's best, and YuanZhang "⁠Attacker⁠" Sheng's role in the coaching team.

The answers in this interview were given in Chinese and translated by HLTV.

What do you think of your performance and result of this event?

I personally am not very satisfied with the result because our goal was actually to make it to the playoffs, but we didn't meet this expectation. And the whole process, I also feel the players didn't perform to the level they should have. There were also a lot of mistakes we made as players, and we weren't in form in some situations.

On map two against Alliance, you lost to a comeback, 3-11. How did it happen? And what did you tell your team after the game?

At that time, it was 11-3. I think the players were a bit relaxed, including the commands given by the IGL as well, and their state of play for this specific match also felt a bit relaxed. They thought we could win this match. After the match, I pointed out the reasons why they came back. It exposed a lot of our problems, for example, in terms of executing tactics, there are always some leaks, and in the mid-game clutch we also played very poorly, including 3v3, 3v2 situations. People were also making individual mistakes.

So what do you think is the biggest problem of your team right now?

The biggest problem of the team is the individual comprehensive ability is not high, and the ability to process information about things happening on the field is not fast enough, not like the current tier one teams' ability to receive information. And then in the mid-game, the ability of dealing clutches is very weak. It's not only in the mechanics, it's also the game sense and the ability to adapt to different situations, and to process different information in a live match. That's our weakness if you compare us to the top teams.

It's not only in the mechanics, it's also the game sense and the ability to adapt to different situations

The four players besides Zero have remained unchanged in the roster for more than two years, which is an unusual longevity for a Counter-Strike roster. Is it because of lack of talent that you did not change, or do you believe in this specific roster and these players?

The former is a bit more, because in China, the player pool is indeed too small. And this might also make the players in our team now have a sense of security, feeling like they won't be replaced so easily, leading them to actually not particularly make efforts to break out of their comfort zone to improve themselves.

What kind of circumstances would lead you to consider changes? Do you have a specific bar or threshold that you need to clear to keep the roster?

There's not a really clear bar with roster changes, or the kind of requirement that we have. But in general, results definitely contribute to this. We need to make sure that we can find the real causes of our failures or of our results, and make sure that it's not always repeating, and we are trying to solve our problems. That's the main goal.

Attacker joined your coaching staff. What is his role in the team?

In usual prep times, Attacker's role is more like disciplinary. He tries to make sure everyone's on point and focused on our work. And also when it comes to officials, he also has some role in our preparation, for himself.
