kyxsan: "In practice I see a different team"

Aurora are yet to start firing on all cylinders with their international lineup, their mixed results in open qualifiers being followed up by a round of 16 exit at Esports World Cup. Now the team have opened their account at BLAST Open Porto with an 0-2 loss at the hands of G2, following a late collapse on Anubis that saw them lose nine rounds in a row.

"They kind of caught us off guard for the first few rounds that we lost," Damjan "⁠kyxsan⁠" Stoilkovski told HLTV of his team's opening defeat in Copenhagen. " I feel like we gave away too many entry kills, so almost every round feels like it's 4v5, and it was a bit hard to play because of that. Usually it doesn't happen in practice."

kyxsan's biggest frustration with his team currently is precisely this fact, that they struggled to translate their play in practice into official matches. "In practice I see different team, different players, different ideas, different people. We just behave differently in practice, and that's one thing that's annoying me right now."

You can listen to the interview here.

A really rough Anubis, you lost a lot of rounds in a row at the end. What went wrong there on Anubis for you?

I just think they played a bit different than they usually do. They kind of caught us off guard for the first few rounds that we lost, and then it was a bit hard for us to adapt. I feel like we gave away too many entry kills, so almost every round feels like it's 4v5, and it was a bit hard to play because of that. Usually it doesn't happen in practice, so it was something new for us. I think that was the main reason.

In general, what are your reflections on that series? Were you expecting it to be a tough one against G2?

Of course G2 is a good team and we are a new team, so we cannot expect to beat G2 comfortably, or even to beat them. I wouldn't say that we are the favorites in this game.

I think we played terrible. It's much different than what we do in practice, and that's why I'm a bit annoyed because in practice I see different team, different players, different ideas, different people. We just behave differently in practice, and that's one thing that's annoying me right now. So the next goal is to just try to repeat everything we do in practice to be able to do it in official.

I think we played terrible

Do you have any idea why it's so hard for you to translate practice into officials?

I'm still not sure because it's only our second LAN tournament, so I still don't know anyone for that long. One thing could be that we are playing a bit differently than the old Aurora, we are trying to have a different way of playing, and we have a different way of preparing for the game. So maybe it's a change for all the players in the team and they need some time to adapt, I guess.

I wanted to ask about that; how has it been for you as the IGL of this new Aurora team? What's it been like for you adapting and creating the new play style?

I think overall it's been good. I would say that practice is going pretty well and I'm able to implement what I want to do in this team, and the players and the coach are trusting me since day one, so that's really important for me. As I said, the results haven't been the best the last few games, but it's a bit hard to play for us because we cannot do everything that we do in practice, as I already said. But overall I would say that I'm happy how we are improving, because I think there's a lot of improvement since, let's say, the first week when we started playing with this team.

How much of the play style is your ideas? How much of it is ashhh? How much of it is the old players? What's the mix like there?

I would say that it's mostly my and ashhh's responsibility, and obviously on the CT side XANTARES is helping a lot. I want every player on my team to have freedom in the team, and they do have it, so if someone wants to create something new or add a new play or a move, everyone is free to do it. The way I want to do things is to just combine this and kind of make a call in the rounds, based on what I see on the server, what I get told from my players and all of that. So I would say that they all have freedom to do things, but obviously the last word is on me and ashhh.

How long do you think you need as a team to get to a good level, to where you want to be?

We haven't really set a time for ourselves, but I would say that it's logical that after a few months maybe we have some expectations. I think right now it's hard to say because, as I said, this is our second LAN tournament. We've been a team for one month and not everything is done properly yet in a way, the playbook is not full, we still don't have too much experience playing together. I would say at least a few months, but it all depends on the players also. Some players adapt faster, some of them slower, so hopefully in a few months we can be much better than now.

Is it easier or harder having such a busy schedule? Obviously you're getting a lot of official reps in, but there's not a lot of time maybe to practice and get a playbook sorted.

I think there's good and bad things about it. I think the good thing is that from playing officials, you learn much more than playing practice because in practice some of the teams play differently and sometimes you cannot just understand what's your level based on the practice games. In the official games I think you get much more experience and you kind of understand what you did right, what you did wrong and all of that. But the bad thing is also that there's so many tournaments in a row sometimes, and sometimes it's good to have some break or to have some time for more theory to create a better playbook. So yeah, as I said, there's good and bad things about it.

Last question then I'll let you go: How challenging is this kind of role for you, being the IGL of the new Aurora, compared to your previous teams like Falcons?

I would say that it's maybe a bit less challenging because I would say that in Falcons, maybe the expectations were a bit higher in a way. But even here, I would say that there's a lot of pressure on us to perform because I still think we have a really good team who... in the past Aurora was top 5, top 10 for a long time. They were a consistent tier one team, so there's also pressure on us to perform. So yeah, I don't think it's easy, but I'm kind of used to it because in Falcons I would say it was a bit worse. I feel like in Falcons the whole community didn't want us to win, here maybe some people do [laughs], so it's a bit easier in a way.
