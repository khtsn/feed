Here's a look at how both matches played out:

After defeating Paper Rex in its final match of the group stage to claim Group Omega's top seed, VARREL retained its step above the dinos, claiming a 2-1 victory in a back-and-forth series. Both teams lost their map picks, which set up for an all-out brawl in the win-or-go-home decider map.

The series started on Paper Rex's pick of Lotus. Both teams went with the Omen-Viper double-Duelist comp with Fade, but the difference was held elsewhere. While PRX went with a Yoru-Raze double-Duelist comp, VARREL opted for Vyse and Raze. To start the map, Paper Rex's defense seemed superior. An early 4-1 lead seized on defense saw Paper Rex take an early lead, but VARREL quickly steadied the boat, evening the score at 6-6 entering halftime. It wouldn't be a bad question to ask if six on attack is enough entering halftime, but VARREL showed off its strong defense. A run of four straight rounds saw VARREL out-duel the opposition and close out the round soon after, 13-6.

After stealing PRX's map pick, VARREL looked poised to close out the series early on Split, arguably one of its best maps throughout its Stage 2 resurgence. Both teams again went with the Omen-Viper-Fade trio, but while VL went with oonzmlp 's Sage and Zexy 's Raze, PRX went with something on Yoru and Jinggg on Raze.

Split could not have been any more back and forth. Neither team won more than three rounds consecutively, trading blows and keeping each other's economies from fully snowballing. That VL-PRX pattern seemed to be near its end when PRX reached map point, but a run of three straight rounds saw VARREL equalize and force overtime. VARREL threatened to close out the series then and there after winning its attack round, but PRX claimed three straight in relatively clean fashion to force a third map. Despite zexy's best efforts in a 28-kill showing on Split, VARREL did not do enough.

Sunset, the decider map, could not have been any more different for the two sides. While VARREL went 2-0 on the map in Stage 2, including one 13-11 win over PRX, PRX was 0-2. Both teams stayed with the same comps they had previously used, with PRX using an Omen-Viper double-Controller pair with Jingg's Raze the lone Duelist, d4v41 's Sage, and something's Sova. Meanwhile, VARREL brought Klaus off of the bench in the Controller role in a double-Sentinel look with Foxy9 's Chamber and oonzmlp's Sage.

Foxy9 gets his clip shut down in one of the most chaotic rounds we've seen all stage.

What started like a runaway map from Paper Rex quickly turned into a well-recovered attack side for VARREL. From down 4-1, the side managed to even out the score by halftime. If zexy was the hero on Split, Foxy9 was the hero on Sunset. He had nine multi-kill rounds, including one ace that could have become a 6K, totaling up 29 kills in the map to help VARREL ride off into a Busan-bound sunset.

VARREL head coach TK9 shared in his postgame media scrum that he had stayed up until 10 a.m. to prepare for the match, a serious gamble on his sleep that has now paid off. TK9 and his former SLT Seongnam SLT Seongnam Korea Rank #3 players will now head to Busan, the same city where they were victorious in the Korean Road to Ascension event last year.

In what could be regarded as Korean Valorant's El Clásico, or at least one of them, T1 and Gen.G went head-to-head with survival on the line. While T1 was bringing in some good form after surviving through the Play-Ins and winning its opening match of the Playoffs' lower bracket, Gen.G showed signs of rust against Nongshim, letting a few tight rounds slip to lose in three maps.

T1, which has enjoyed a lot of success on Lotus's defense side with its current comp, entered halftime up 7-5, and was flawless on attack, closing out the map with a smooth 13-5.

Between maps, Gen.G head coach solo shared that the team had struggled going up against comps with Viper and Chamber on Lotus, which may have hinted at what went wrong. T1 opted for a switchup of its comp from earlier in the stage, going with a Yoru-Raze Duelist duo with Fade, Omen, and Jett as opposed to a Raze-Jett comp with Skye.

BuZz's 4K spells the beginning of the end for Gen.G.

Heroics from BuZz, who wrapped up a 4K to reach match point, were followed up by a Gen.G round that was built from nearly scraps due to a decimated economy. While Gen.G did claw back one round, it was ultimately too little, too late, as T1 punched its ticket to Busan and kept its Shanghai dream alive.

iZu finished with a 1.37 rating, the highest in the series, while BuZz and Meteor's 38 and 37 kills carried the fragging load for T1 in the win. t3xture's 32 kills across both maps were not enough on this day, as was Raxcal 's team-high 0.97.

For former Gen.G players Munchkin and Meteor, the quest to return to Shanghai, where they lifted a Masters trophy two years ago with the Tigers, is still alive.
