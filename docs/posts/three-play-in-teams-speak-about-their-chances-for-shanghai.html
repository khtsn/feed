Three play-in teams speak about their chances for Shanghai

Ascension is no more in 2026, but the VCT Stage 2 Play-Ins now give tier-two teams their shot to play against tier-one squads. If these rosters are strong enough, they'll qualify for Champions 2026 in Shanghai and continue to compete at the highest level.

Each region has presented worthy competitors, but the mountain to climb is massive. The format makes the journey from tier-two to Shanghai difficult, with teams needing to finish top four in the Play-Ins to then end top two in the playoffs.

After qualifying for the play-ins on his birthday, Kaplan was excited for the grind. The added bit of motivation could be to compete against his former squad in Sentinels, but he's just happy to be back in the saddle again against any tier-one org.

“I'm grateful to play against any team in tier-one,” Kaplan said. “I know the world is really excited for us to potentially play Sentinels, and I know the guys would have a lot of fun.”

Ignoring specific teams, the back-to-back tournaments are enough of a challenge on their own. All play-in teams have to fight through two tournaments to make it to Shanghai, and while that might seem like a daunting task, the Madrid winner can't wait.

“I enjoy the tournament format because of the tight turnaround; I like the pressure, and I think that's why I'm successful in those spots,” Kaplan said. “I really, really enjoy the non-stop games. I missed it, and while the games might be tighter in those back-to-backs, I'll be able to help the team a lot because of it.”

“Rather than being relaxed, I want it more,” Hype said. “To be honest, I've always taken a challenger mindset, always looking toward the next step. I've always tried to be a challenger as much as possible, and that's no different now for play-ins.”

The gauntlet every team faces in each play-in is a chance at tier-one, but these squads face tougher opposition than the previous Ascension tournaments. Before, it was a scrappy battle between the best in tier-two, but those battles also consistently left the question of: How many of these teams could truly beat the worst in tier-one?

These play-ins provide that answer. Ascension and LCQs have morphed together into a hybrid month-long stretch, and the top of the mountain is well within reach for those who truly deserve it.
